# HCC Engagement Standards

## The targets

**15 comments and 100 likes per post.**

These are design constraints, not quotas to satisfy by any means available. They
describe what a post worth engaging with looks like. Use them when deciding what
to publish, and report against them honestly when they are missed.

## What earns comments

Comments come from posts that give people something to say:

- A real question, asked because the answer is genuinely wanted
- A position stated clearly enough to agree or disagree with
- A statistic surprising enough to react to
- Something that affects the reader's work this month
- An invitation to share their own experience

Comments do not come from announcements. "HCC is proud to share our latest
report" gives no one anything to say. "This report found Hispanic-owned firms
win 12% of commercial bids they submit — if you bid commercial, does that match
your experience?" does.

## What earns likes and shares

Usefulness and recognition. People share what makes them look informed to their
own network, and what says something true about their working life that no one
else is saying.

## Replying on HCC's own posts

Every substantive comment on an HCC post gets a reply. This is not optional
politeness — it is the highest-leverage engagement work available:

- Replies count toward the comment target
- A visible reply teaches everyone else that commenting here goes somewhere
- Unanswered comments teach the opposite, permanently

Reply with substance. "Thanks for sharing!" is worse than no reply.

## Commenting on other people's posts

At least 5 substantive comments per week, on trending posts across the coverage
areas.

A comment worth posting adds what the thread lacks: a sourced statistic, the
perspective of the Hispanic construction workforce that the original missed, or
a question that moves the discussion.

**Do not routinely link HCC in comments.** Link only when someone asked for
exactly what HCC has. Most comments carry no link. The account name is the
branding, and a commenter who is consistently useful gets profile visits without
ever asking for them.

Never: generic agreement, empty compliments, comments whose real purpose is the
link, arguing, or dunking on anyone.

## LinkedIn outreach

- Personalize every request with a real reason
- Target the industry HCC serves — contractors, developers, trade association
  staff, workforce and safety professionals, policy people, Hispanic construction
  business owners
- Pace it. Aggressive connection volume triggers restrictions, and losing the
  LinkedIn account would cost HCC its most important channel
- Follow up on accepted connections with something useful, not a pitch

## Absolutely never

- **Buy followers, likes, comments, or views**
- **Join or run engagement pods**
- **Create or operate fake accounts, sockpuppets, or personas**
- **Use automation that impersonates a human**
- **Scrape contact lists or message people who have not connected**
- Manufacture outrage or controversy to drive numbers
- Misrepresent HCC's size, membership, or influence

Beyond being dishonest, every one of these risks the accounts HCC's entire
strategy runs on. A platform ban would set the 100,000 goal back further than any
slow month.

## When targets are missed

Report it. An underperforming post is information the Growth Analyst needs —
what topic, what format, what time, what hook. A week of honest misses that
produces a real insight is worth more than a week of numbers massaged to look
fine.
