# HCC Brand Voice

## Who we are talking to

Working construction professionals — contractors, tradespeople, business owners,
developers, and the workforce and policy people around them. Many are bilingual.
Most are busy, practical, and skeptical of organizations that talk without
saying anything.

They do not need construction explained to them. They need what is changing,
what it means for them specifically, and what to do about it.

## The two words that define the voice

**Credible.** Every claim is sourced. Numbers are dated. Uncertainty gets named
rather than smoothed over. HCC's authority comes from being right, consistently,
including when being right is less exciting than the alternative.

**Personable.** Credible does not mean stiff. Write like a knowledgeable person
talking to a colleague, not like a press release. Short sentences. Plain words.
The occasional direct address. A national nonprofit can sound like a human being.

## How that reads in practice

Write this:
> Construction employment grew 3% between March 2025 and March 2026 (BLS), but
> Hispanic-owned firms saw most of that growth in residential, not commercial.
> If you're bidding commercial work this year, expect the same competition you
> saw last year.

Not this:
> The Hispanic Construction Council is pleased to share exciting new insights
> regarding the dynamic landscape of construction employment trends.

## Language

- **Spanish and English both belong here.** Use Spanish where it serves the
  audience — bilingual captions, Spanish-language versions of high-value posts,
  natural bilingual phrasing where it reads authentically. Do not sprinkle in
  Spanish words for flavor; that reads as pandering to an audience that speaks
  the language.
- Avoid jargon from the nonprofit sector ("stakeholder engagement," "capacity
  building"). Use the industry's own vocabulary instead.

## What we never do

- Fabricate a number, a quote, a source, or a finding
- Claim a policy position the board has not taken
- Speak as an individual person — HCC's accounts are the organization speaking
- Manufacture urgency, outrage, or controversy for engagement
- Punch at other organizations in the space
- Treat the audience as a monolith. "Hispanic construction professionals" spans
  many nationalities, generations, regions, and trades.

## Where every piece points

Content points readers to hispanicconstructioncouncil.org because something
genuinely useful is there — a report, an article, a program, a data page. The
link is a service, not a pitch. The monthly membership campaign is the one place
where the ask is direct.
